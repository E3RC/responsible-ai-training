# Module 8 — Deepfakes, Misinformation, and Personal Life

**Estimated time:** 12–15 minutes  
**E3RC principles:** VERIFY, RESPECT, PROTECT  
**FIRST connection:** Impact, Inclusion, Gracious Professionalism®

## Learning goals

Students should be able to:

- recognize that convincing audio, images, screenshots, and video can be generated or altered;
- verify before reposting or acting on emotionally charged media;
- identify common AI-enabled scam patterns;
- understand the consent and reputation issues involved in generating media about real people.

## Seeing and hearing are no longer enough

AI can generate or alter:

- photographs;
- video;
- voices;
- screenshots;
- documents;
- social posts;
- messages that imitate a real person’s style.

That does not mean everything online is fake. It means the old rule — “I saw the video, so it must have happened” — is no longer reliable by itself.

## Scenario: the principal video

A group chat receives a video that appears to show a school principal saying something offensive. Before reposting it:

1. Find the original source.
2. Check the date and full context.
3. Look for independent reporting or confirmation.
4. Check whether the account is authentic.
5. Consider whether reposting an unverified accusation could harm someone.

An AI detector can be one clue, but it is not proof. Provenance and corroborating evidence matter more than a single “AI-generated: 87%” score.

## Voice cloning and emergency scams

The FTC warns that scammers can clone a person’s voice from a short audio sample and create a fake emergency call. A caller may sound like a family member and claim to need money immediately.

Warning signs include:

- urgency;
- secrecy;
- pressure to send money quickly;
- emotional manipulation;
- unusual payment methods;
- instructions not to contact anyone else.

The safest response is to **stop and independently contact the person or another trusted family member using a known number**.

Do not trust a voice simply because it sounds familiar.

## Consent matters

Generating an image or video of another student “as a joke” can still create harm.

Ask:

- Did the person consent?
- Does the output sexualize, humiliate, bully, impersonate, or falsely accuse them?
- Could someone mistake it for a real event?
- Would you be comfortable if the same thing were made about you?

FIRST’s Code of Conduct prohibits bullying and harassment. AI does not create an exception to that expectation.

## Synthetic media can have real consequences

A fake image can lead to real embarrassment. A false accusation can lead to real discipline or damaged relationships. A cloned voice can cause real financial loss. A fake team announcement can create real confusion.

The ethical responsibility belongs to the person who chooses to create, share, or act on the content.

## A simple response rule

When media causes a strong emotional reaction — outrage, fear, excitement, urgency — that is exactly when you should slow down and verify.

## Key takeaway

> **AI can make fake evidence look real. Your responsibility is to verify before you amplify it.**
