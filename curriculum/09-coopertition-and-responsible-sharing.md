# Module 9 — Coopertition, Sharing, and Responsible AI

**Estimated time:** 10–12 minutes  
**E3RC principles:** RESPECT, VERIFY, OWN IT  
**FIRST connection:** Coopertition®, Gracious Professionalism®, Teamwork

## Learning goals

Students should be able to:

- apply FIRST values when AI is used to help teammates or competitors;
- distinguish useful sharing from unsafe or misleading sharing;
- communicate uncertainty honestly;
- protect private information while still supporting the FIRST community.

## AI changes how quickly we can help

A student can now ask an AI assistant for troubleshooting steps, wiring ideas, code examples, rule explanations, and design concepts in seconds. That makes it easier to help another team — and easier to spread a bad answer quickly.

FIRST describes Coopertition as teams helping and cooperating with one another even while they compete. Responsible AI use fits this well: **use the tool to increase what you can share, but do not turn an unverified guess into confident advice.**

## Scenario: another team’s robot is down

A neighboring team asks for help with an electrical issue. An AI suggests a fix that seems plausible.

The responsible response is not:

> “AI says this will fix it.”

A better response is:

> “Here’s one possible cause. We have not verified it yet. Let’s check the wiring diagram and vendor documentation together.”

That combines speed, honesty, and safety.

## Gracious Professionalism is a compass

FIRST leadership has described Gracious Professionalism as a **compass for guiding your own actions**, not a yardstick for attacking other people. That is useful in AI ethics too.

The purpose of this training is not to catch teammates using AI “wrong” so you can shame them. It is to help the team develop habits that make everyone’s work stronger, safer, and more trustworthy.

If you see a teammate relying on an unverified AI answer, help them verify it. If someone accidentally pastes a secret into an AI tool, help contain the problem and rotate the credential rather than turning it into gossip.

## What should be shared?

FIRST culture rewards knowledge-sharing, but not every piece of information should be posted publicly.

Before sharing AI-assisted material, consider:

- Is it accurate?
- Is it safe?
- Does it contain credentials or private student information?
- Does it expose confidential sponsor or organizational information?
- Is it someone else’s copyrighted or private material?
- Are we clear about what is confirmed versus experimental?

## Open knowledge and attribution

When Team 1555 publishes documentation, code, training resources, or examples, attribution and licensing can make sharing easier and clearer.

This AI ethics curriculum itself is designed to be public so other teams can learn from and improve it. That is Coopertition in practice: build something useful, document it honestly, and make it easier for others to benefit.

## Competition does not excuse deception

It would violate the spirit of FIRST to intentionally provide another team with false AI-generated information, unsafe advice, or misleading rule interpretations just to gain an advantage.

Compete hard. Help honestly.

## Key takeaway

> **Coopertition means sharing knowledge generously. Responsible AI means making sure what you share is honest, safe, and clear about uncertainty.**
