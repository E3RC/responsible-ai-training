# Module 10 — Responsible AI Commitment and Final Assessment

**Estimated time:** 18–22 minutes  
**E3RC principles:** VERIFY, PROTECT, DISCLOSE, RESPECT, OWN IT  
**FIRST connection:** All FIRST Core Values

## Learning goals

Students should be able to bring the entire course together and apply responsible AI principles in unfamiliar situations.

## The Responsible AI Five

### VERIFY
AI output is not automatically true. Check important claims against current, authoritative sources.

### PROTECT
Do not casually give AI systems private, sensitive, secret, or unnecessary information.

### DISCLOSE
Be honest about when and how AI materially assisted your work when rules or context call for disclosure.

### RESPECT
Do not use AI to deceive, bully, impersonate, exploit, harass, or unfairly diminish the work of others.

### OWN IT
You remain responsible for what you submit, publish, build, wire, run, or act upon — even when AI helped create it.

## Before the final: the 30-second check

When you are unsure whether an AI use is responsible, ask:

1. **What am I trying to accomplish?**
2. **What could go wrong?**
3. **Whose information, work, safety, or reputation is involved?**
4. **What rule or authoritative source applies?**
5. **Would I be comfortable explaining my process to a teacher, mentor, teammate, parent, inspector, or affected person?**
6. **Who is accountable for the final result?**

Most real ethical decisions are not solved by memorizing one list of forbidden tools. They are solved by recognizing consequences, checking rules, respecting people, and staying accountable.

## Final assessment design

The certification final should:

- draw **20 questions** randomly from the full question bank;
- include questions from every major topic area;
- use a mixture of single-choice and select-all-that-apply questions;
- require **80%** to pass;
- show topic-level remediation after an unsuccessful attempt;
- allow a retry after review;
- avoid showing a complete static answer key before completion.

The purpose is mastery, not punishment.

## Student commitment

Before the certificate is issued, students affirm:

> **As a member of E3 Robotics Center and FIRST Robotics Competition Team 1555, I will use artificial intelligence to expand my abilities — not replace my responsibility. I will verify important information, protect private information, respect the work and dignity of others, disclose AI assistance when appropriate, and remain accountable for the work I submit, publish, build, or deploy.**

## Certificate requirements

A passing student receives the **E3RC Responsible Artificial Intelligence Certification** with:

- student name;
- E3 Robotics Center;
- FRC Team 1555;
- completion date;
- final score;
- curriculum version;
- unique certificate ID;
- QR code to a verification page.

The certificate must state that it is an E3 Robotics Center credential and **not an official FIRST-issued certification**.

## What this certification means

It does **not** mean a student will never make a mistake with AI.

It means the student has demonstrated a baseline understanding of:

- AI limitations and verification;
- responsible school use;
- engineering accountability;
- teamwork and authorship;
- bias and inclusion;
- privacy and security;
- synthetic media and misinformation;
- responsible sharing and Coopertition.

## Final thought

FIRST exists to prepare young people for the future. AI will be part of that future. The goal is not to make Team 1555 afraid of powerful tools. The goal is to develop students who can use powerful tools **with judgment, skill, curiosity, integrity, and responsibility.**
