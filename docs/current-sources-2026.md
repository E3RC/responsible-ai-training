# Current Source Register — 2026.1

**Reviewed:** August 21, 2026

This file records the primary current sources used to keep the E3RC Responsible AI curriculum grounded in up-to-date guidance. Curriculum authors should re-check these sources before each annual release.

## FIRST

### FIRST Mission, Purpose, Core Values, Gracious Professionalism®, and Coopertition®
https://www.firstinspires.org/about

Current FIRST Core Values: **Discovery, Innovation, Impact, Inclusion, Teamwork, Fun**. FIRST describes Gracious Professionalism as encouraging high-quality work, valuing others, and respecting individuals and the community; Coopertition encourages teams to help and cooperate even while competing.

### FIRST Youth Protection Program and Code of Conduct
https://www.firstinspires.org/programs/youth-protection-program

Relevant expectations include Gracious Professionalism, participant safety, and prohibitions on bullying and harassment.

### FIRST Robotics Competition Awards
https://www.firstinspires.org/resources/library/frc/awards

Current award guidance states that teams are expected to follow FIRST Core Values, demonstrate Gracious Professionalism, and implement appropriate safety practices.

### Gracious Professionalism and Coopertition — Collin Fultz, FIRST Robotics Competition
https://community.firstinspires.org/gracious-professionalism-and-coopertition

Useful framing: Gracious Professionalism as a compass for guiding one's own actions rather than a yardstick for judging others.

## Indiana education

### Indiana Department of Education — Computer Science
https://www.in.gov/doe/students/computer-science/

As of August 2026, IDOE explicitly identifies AI as an emerging computer-science discipline and says students should understand what AI is, how it works, and its societal implications. IDOE frames students as critical consumers, responsible creators, and informed participants in a computing-powered world.

Indiana also notes the computer-science graduation requirement beginning with the graduating class of 2029.

### Indiana Department of Education — Digital Learning
https://www.in.gov/doe/educators/digital-learning/

Includes Indiana's AI guidance for K–12 education and information from AI platform pilots.

## U.S. Department of Education

### Artificial Intelligence Guidance
https://www.ed.gov/about/ed-overview/artificial-intelligence-ai-guidance

Page reviewed February 10, 2026. Federal guidance supports responsible and secure AI use and innovation.

### July 22, 2025 AI in Schools Guidance Announcement
https://www.ed.gov/about/news/press-release/us-department-of-education-issues-guidance-artificial-intelligence-use-schools-proposes-additional-supplemental-priority

Highlights responsible adoption, AI literacy, privacy, appropriate AI use in social media, and stakeholder/parent engagement.

### K–12 Cybersecurity
https://www.ed.gov/teaching-and-administration/safe-learning-environments/school-safety-and-security/k-12-cybersecurity

Reviewed March 17, 2026. Recommends a practical “Core 4”: long unique passwords (preferably with a password manager), MFA, software updates, and alertness for scams.

### Protecting Student Privacy
https://studentprivacy.ed.gov/

Current FERPA/PPRA guidance and resources. Schools are advised to review online tools and services before sharing personally identifiable information from education records and to consider security/privacy vulnerabilities.

## International AI literacy

### UNESCO AI Competency Framework for Students
https://unesdoc.unesco.org/

Framework emphasizes responsible and creative citizenship, human-centered thinking, AI ethics, foundations/applications, and system design.

## Risk and trustworthy AI

### NIST AI Risk Management Framework
https://www.nist.gov/itl/ai-risk-management-framework

Provides a risk-based framework for identifying and managing AI trustworthiness concerns such as reliability, safety, privacy, fairness, transparency, and accountability.

## Scams and synthetic media

### Federal Trade Commission — Fake Emergency Scams and Voice Cloning
https://consumer.ftc.gov/articles/scammers-use-fake-emergencies-steal-your-money

FTC warns that scammers can use short audio clips and voice-cloning tools to impersonate family or friends. Recommended response is to independently verify through a known contact route rather than trusting the voice.

## Copyright and AI

### U.S. Copyright Office — Copyright and Artificial Intelligence
https://www.copyright.gov/ai/

Part 2 of the Office's report addresses copyrightability of AI-assisted and AI-generated outputs. The Office continues to center human authorship; assistive use of AI does not automatically destroy copyright protection, while purely AI-generated expressive material raises different copyrightability issues.

**Teaching note:** This curriculum should not attempt to provide legal advice. Students should learn the practical distinction between creating, copying, licensing, attribution, and human authorship, and should check current rules when the legal status matters.

## Update policy

Before releasing a new curriculum version:

1. Re-check FIRST Core Values, Code of Conduct, FRC award/safety guidance, and the current season manual.
2. Re-check Indiana Department of Education AI resources.
3. Re-check U.S. Department of Education AI, privacy, and cybersecurity guidance.
4. Re-check FTC synthetic-media/scam guidance.
5. Re-check the U.S. Copyright Office AI reports and major legal changes.
6. Review at least one current youth-facing AI-literacy framework.
7. Record the review date in this document and increment the curriculum version when substantive content changes.
